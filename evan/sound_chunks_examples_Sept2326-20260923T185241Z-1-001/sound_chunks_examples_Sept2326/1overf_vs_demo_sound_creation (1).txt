COMPARISON OF THE TWO 500-ms BASE SOUNDS
===========================================

This file describes the two synthetic 500-ms base sounds used for the scrambled-chunk examples:

1. The "1/f naturalistic" dynamic tone complex
2. The original "demo intact" synthetic sound


------------------------------------------------------------
1. 1/f NATURALISTIC DYNAMIC TONE COMPLEX
------------------------------------------------------------

Purpose
-------
This sound was designed as a controlled naturalistic stimulus with broadband spectral content and temporally structured amplitude and frequency modulation. Its temporal modulation statistics follow an approximate 1/f power spectrum.

Duration and sampling
---------------------
Duration: 500 ms
Sampling rate: 192 kHz

Carrier components
------------------
The carrier is a sum of 10 equal-power sinusoidal components spanning approximately 4-32 kHz:

4.00 kHz
5.04 kHz
6.35 kHz
8.00 kHz
10.08 kHz
12.70 kHz
16.00 kHz
20.16 kHz
25.40 kHz
32.00 kHz

The components are summed with 1/sqrt(10) scaling so that total power does not simply grow with the number of tones.

Each component is assigned an independent random initial phase.

1/f modulation trajectories
----------------------------
Two independent frozen random modulation trajectories are generated:

a(t): amplitude-modulation trajectory
q(t): frequency-modulation trajectory

For each trajectory, a random signal is constructed in the Fourier domain.

Only modulation frequencies between 2 and 80 Hz are retained.

For each retained Fourier frequency f, the Fourier amplitude is set proportional to:

    1 / sqrt(f)

with a random phase.

Because power is proportional to squared Fourier amplitude, this gives an approximate modulation power spectrum:

    P(f) proportional to 1/f

The inverse FFT gives a real-valued temporal modulation trajectory.

Each trajectory is then rescaled to the range [-1, 1].

Amplitude modulation
--------------------
The AM trajectory controls instantaneous sound level according to:

    L(t) = 60 + 10*a(t) dB

Since a(t) lies between -1 and +1, the relative level trajectory spans approximately:

    50 to 70 dB

The corresponding linear amplitude multiplier is:

    A(t) = 10^[(L(t)-60)/20]

Thus the waveform undergoes slow and fast amplitude fluctuations whose temporal modulation spectrum follows approximately 1/f between 2 and 80 Hz.

Frequency modulation
--------------------
The independent FM trajectory q(t) coherently shifts the entire tone complex in frequency.

For carrier k with base frequency f_k0:

    f_k(t) = f_k0 * 2^[0.30*q(t)]

This produces a coherent spectral shift of up to approximately:

    +/- 0.30 octave

All 10 carrier frequencies move together, preserving the ratios between carrier frequencies.

The instantaneous frequency trajectory is integrated over time to obtain phase before generating each sinusoid.

Final waveform
--------------
The final carrier sum is multiplied by the AM envelope:

    s(t) = A(t) * (1/sqrt(10)) * sum_k sin(phi_k(t))

A 5-ms onset ramp and 5-ms offset ramp are applied.

For demonstration playback, the waveform is normalized to a fixed digital level. Absolute SPL in an experiment would still need to be set using speaker calibration.

Conceptual interpretation
-------------------------
This is not ordinary pink noise.

It is a structured dynamic tone complex in which:

- the amplitude envelope has approximately 1/f temporal statistics
- the coherent spectral motion also has approximately 1/f temporal statistics
- spectral structure is explicitly constrained to the chosen carrier frequencies

The goal is to provide controlled but naturalistic temporal structure.


------------------------------------------------------------
2. ORIGINAL "DEMO INTACT" SYNTHETIC SOUND
------------------------------------------------------------

Purpose
-------
This sound was originally made simply as an easy-to-hear demonstration of chunk scrambling. It was designed to contain obvious changing temporal and spectral structure.

It does NOT use a 1/f modulation process.

Duration and sampling
---------------------
Duration: 500 ms
Sampling rate: 48 kHz

Waveform components
-------------------
The waveform is the weighted sum of three components.

Component 1: logarithmic upward chirp
-------------------------------------
Weight: 0.45

Frequency trajectory:

    1.2 kHz -> 9 kHz

over 500 ms, using a logarithmic chirp.

Component 2: linear downward chirp
----------------------------------
Weight: 0.25

Frequency trajectory:

    8.5 kHz -> 1.8 kHz

over 500 ms, using a linear chirp.

Component 3: sinusoidal FM component
------------------------------------
Weight: 0.18

The base term is approximately centered near 3.5 kHz, with a sinusoidal frequency modulation driven at approximately 3 Hz.

The implementation was:

    sin(2*pi*(3500*t + 700*sin(2*pi*3*t)/(2*pi)))

This produces a smoothly changing FM tone.

Amplitude envelope
------------------
The summed waveform is multiplied by a nonstationary amplitude envelope made from two low-frequency sinusoidal modulations:

    2.1 Hz
    5.3 Hz

with different phases and amplitudes.

The envelope was:

    env(t) =
        0.35
        + 0.65 * [
            0.5
            + 0.28*sin(2*pi*2.1*t + 0.3)
            + 0.17*sin(2*pi*5.3*t + 1.2)
        ]

The envelope is clipped to stay within a positive range.

Unlike the 1/f sound, this amplitude trajectory is deterministic and consists of only a small number of sinusoidal modulation components.

Outer ramps and normalization
-----------------------------
A 5-ms onset ramp and 5-ms offset ramp are applied.

The waveform is then peak-normalized to approximately:

    -3 dBFS

This gives the original demo sound a relatively strong average level and an immediately audible sweeping/chirping character.


------------------------------------------------------------
3. MAIN DIFFERENCES
------------------------------------------------------------

1/f naturalistic sound
----------------------
- 10 simultaneous tone carriers spanning 4-32 kHz
- independent frozen 1/f AM and FM trajectories
- modulation frequencies distributed across 2-80 Hz
- AM temporal power approximately follows 1/f
- FM temporal power approximately follows 1/f
- all carrier frequencies shift coherently during FM
- stochastic but reproducible for a fixed random seed
- designed as a controlled naturalistic stimulus

Original demo sound
-------------------
- mixture of two chirps plus one FM sinusoidal component
- deterministic spectral trajectories
- amplitude modulation built from two sinusoids
- no 1/f modulation statistics
- strong obvious sweeping structure
- designed primarily to make temporal scrambling easy to hear


------------------------------------------------------------
4. WHY THEY SOUND DIFFERENT
------------------------------------------------------------

The demo sound has strong, obvious directional sweeps and a relatively simple envelope. Human listeners can easily hear when those trajectories are broken up and reordered.

The 1/f naturalistic sound contains many simultaneous frequencies and stochastic AM/FM fluctuations. Its structure is richer and less dominated by a single audible sweep.

The two sounds therefore test somewhat different kinds of temporal structure:

- The demo sound emphasizes obvious trajectory and sequence information.
- The 1/f sound emphasizes broadband, multiscale temporal structure with controlled 1/f statistics.


------------------------------------------------------------
5. SCRAMBLING
------------------------------------------------------------

For both sound types, the scrambling procedure can use the same basic logic:

1. Start with the same 500-ms base waveform.
2. Divide it into equal chunks, for example:
       125 ms    -> 4 chunks
       62.5 ms   -> 8 chunks
       31.25 ms  -> 16 chunks
       15.625 ms -> 32 chunks
3. Reorder those chunks without replacement.
4. Preserve the waveform inside each chunk.
5. Optionally insert short gaps or apply boundary crossfades.
6. RMS-match the final stimuli so that differences in neural response are not trivially explained by differences in overall sound level.

Thus, across chunk sizes, the main intended manipulation is the temporal scale at which sequence order is disrupted.
